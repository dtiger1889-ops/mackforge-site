Portal walkthrough · Appendices

How the game works

Spoilers. This page shows everything in it, puzzle solutions included, and sometimes points ahead to later chambers.

How the game’s systems work, and what each hazard does. The portal gun itself is covered in Items and objects, and the story side of things is in Story notes.

Portal 2 mix-ups to avoid

The original Portal does NOT contain Aerial Faith Plates, Thermal Discouragement Beams (lasers), gels, Hard Light Bridges, or Excursion Funnels. Those are all Portal 2. The original game’s systems are limited to portals, buttons, Weighted/Companion Cubes, High Energy Pellets, Unstationary Scaffolds, goo, Emancipation Grills, Sentry Turrets, the rocket turret, and personality cores.

Portals

Two linked portals. The ASHPD fires two linked portals, blue and orange. Only one of each color exists at a time, and firing a new blue portal moves the existing blue one. You and objects pass through one aperture and exit the other instantaneously.†

Which surfaces take a portal. Portals attach only to flat, light-colored, concrete-type surfaces. Dark metallic brown or black panels are non-portalable, and glass and moving surfaces reject portals.† The developer commentary reportedly explains the dark colour as a deliberate choice, so non-portalable surfaces are easy to spot from a distance; this is not confirmed.†

Crosshair feedback. The crosshair indicator changes colour when it is over a valid portal-placement surface. It is the primary read for spotting portalable surfaces and edge placements.†

When you get the gun. Test Chamber 00 and Test Chamber 01 have no player gun, only automated portals. You retrieve the blue-only ASHPD in Test Chamber 02. The fully powered dual-portal (blue and orange) device comes in Test Chamber 11, and it triggers “Lab Rat”.

Momentum and flinging

The core rule. Momentum is conserved. Exit speed equals entry speed, redirected along the exit portal’s surface normal. GLaDOS states it in Test Chamber 10: “Momentum, a function of mass and velocity, is conserved between portals. In layman’s terms, speedy thing goes in, speedy thing comes out.”

Fall into fling. Falling into a floor portal and exiting a wall portal flings you horizontally at the speed you reached falling. This underlies most advanced solutions from Test Chamber 10 onward.

Engine constants. Gravity is governed by sv_gravity, which defaults to 600 in Portal (Valve’s Source default is 800 in other games). sv_maxvelocity caps ballistic speed at 3500 per axis, not total magnitude, so diagonal flings can exceed 3500 total.†

Air-strafe. Portal 1 allows air-strafing during a fling to bend the arc, with more air control than Portal 2.†

Edge cases. On non-parallel portals you are reoriented upright to gravity after exit. The edge-glitch, or portal-ledging, lets a portal sit on otherwise-invalid edges.†

Portal bumping. Placing a portal onto another bumps the other portal aside, next to the one you just placed. It can be used to skip certain chambers.†

Speedrun note: Accelerated Back Hopping. The dominant movement tech is ABH, a Source/OrangeBox glitch replacing the patched-out bunny hop. New speed per perfect hop is about (current speed x 2 - speed limit), and the gain is exponential. Per the SourceRuns Wiki, the speed limit is 165 UPS while crouched in Portal specifically (versus 209 elsewhere), 225 walking, 285 running, and 352 sprinting. It was discovered by Spider-Waffle.†

Falling speed after death. What happens to fall velocity on death and respawn is not documented in the sources reviewed.†

Emancipation Grills

What they do. They vaporize unauthorized objects (cubes, turrets, cameras, radios, rockets), block portal shots, and reset your active portals on pass-through. You and the ASHPD pass through unharmed.

Pass or dissolve. The test subject and portal gun pass. Cubes, turrets, cameras, radios, and rockets are vaporized, and a dissolving turret emits a long “owowowow”. Portals cannot be placed through or across a grill, and portals on the far side are unreachable. High Energy Pellets are destroyed on contact like other objects.

One exception. Some square metal maintenance panels are NOT affected by the grill, a developer oversight.†

Names. StrategyWiki calls it the “Material Emancipation Grid”, also “emancipation grills”.† GLaDOS says “Grill” in-game.† The subtitles and the Prima guide reportedly both use “Grid”; this is not confirmed.†

Hazards

Sentry Turrets (first seen in Test Chamber 16). They detect your line-of-sight, deploy laser sights, then open fire. You can pick one up with the portal gun and topple it. The entrance glass in Test Chamber 16 is bulletproof. Tactics: place a portal behind or under a turret to topple it. For alcove turrets you can’t portal under, place a portal on the red X above them and drop a cube, camera, or turret onto it. Waiting for a turret’s laser to settle gives more reaction time.†

Toxic goo and acid (first seen in Test Chamber 08). It is an instant-kill floor hazard. Test Chamber 08 was originally meant to be the first pellet chamber, but playtesters found too many mechanics at once, so Test Chamber 06 and Test Chamber 07 were inserted before it.†

High Energy Pellets (first seen in Test Chamber 06). A bouncing energy ball that kills in one hit. Route it through a portal into a receiver to power lifts and doors. The receiver shines a red light on the ceiling to help aim the portal. Pellets recharge their lifespan each time they pass through a portal, extending the window before they disintegrate. Otherwise they fade out after a few bounces. A green, non-disintegrating variant of the High Energy Pellet appears in Test Chamber 19.†

Unstationary Scaffolds (first seen in Test Chamber 07). A moving platform you ride, typically combined with pellet routing.†

Objects

Weighted Storage Cubes. The primary puzzle prop. Put one on a button to hold a door or mechanism open. Cubes can be carried through portals, and they work as height boosts and turret-drop weapons.

The Weighted Companion Cube (Test Chamber 17). A special named cube that functions identically to a normal Weighted Storage Cube. It must be incinerated in the Incinerator Room to open the chamber exit, which triggers “Fratricide”. The story side is in Story notes.

Buttons. Large floor buttons hold-to-activate and need a cube or your weight. There are also wall buttons.
