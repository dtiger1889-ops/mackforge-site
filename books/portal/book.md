## Test Chamber 00 {#ch-01 .first-in-part}

> **Collectibles here:**
>
> - Radio (1): on the table in the Relaxation Vault, the room you wake up in. <!-- f:chamber_00-f05 --> On your first playthrough it counts for nothing, because the Transmission Received achievement only starts counting radios after you've finished the game once. <!-- f:chamber_00-f05 -->

### Walkthrough

1. You wake inside a glass-walled vault that looks like a small bedroom, with a radio playing music on the table and a countdown timer mounted above the spot where a portal is about to open. <!-- f:chamber_00-f07 -->
2. When the countdown ends, a portal opens in the vault wall. <!-- f:chamber_00-f08 --> Step through and you come out outside the vault, facing its glass; there's no way back in. <!-- f:chamber_00-f08 -->
3. Walk toward the main room, and a Weighted Storage Cube drops from a ceiling dispenser, the Vital Apparatus Vent. <!-- f:chamber_00-f09 -->
4. Carry the cube to the large round floor button, the Heavy Duty Super-Colliding Super Button, and set it down: the exit door opens. <!-- f:chamber_00-f06 f:chamber_00-f10 -->
5. Leave the cube behind and walk through the glowing particle field across the exit. <!-- f:chamber_00-f11 --> This is the Material Emancipation Grill: it vaporizes anything you carry, so the cube can't come with you, and you can't walk back through it. <!-- f:chamber_00-f11 -->
6. **Exit.** Test Chamber 00 ends at the chamberlock elevator, and the ride to [Test Chamber 01](#ch-02) is one-way. <!-- f:chamber_00-f02 f:chamber_00-f03 f:chamber_00-f12 -->

> **Tip:** Nothing in this chamber can hurt you, so you don't need a quicksave. <!-- f:chamber_00-f13 --> The only automatic save is the one at the start; none was found partway through. <!-- f:chamber_00-f13 -->

## Test Chamber 01 {#ch-02}

There's still no portal gun in Test Chamber 01. <!-- f:chamber_01-f04 --> Instead, a single portal changes where it leads all by itself, and the whole test is watching its pattern before you move. <!-- f:chamber_01-f04 --> Your job is to get the cube into the button room through that cycling portal, then reach the exit room the same way. <!-- f:chamber_01-f06 -->

> **Collectibles here:**
>
> - Radio (1): sitting on top of a camera unit beside the entrance, ready to grab the moment you arrive. <!-- f:chamber_01-f05 f:chamber_01-f07 --> Take it before you drop into the pit, because the drop (gate 1 on the map) is one-way. <!-- f:chamber_01-f05 f:chamber_01-f07 -->

### Walkthrough

1. From the entrance ledge you look down over the main pit. <!-- f:chamber_01-f07 --> Pick up the radio if you want it, then drop in. <!-- f:chamber_01-f07 -->
2. Watch the orange portal: it cycles between three sealed side rooms, a cube room, a button room and an exit room. <!-- f:chamber_01-f08 --> You go in through the matching blue side, timing it for the room you want. <!-- f:chamber_01-f08 -->
3. When it shows the cube room, go through and pick up the Weighted Storage Cube. <!-- f:chamber_01-f09 -->
4. When it shows the button room, carry the cube in and put it on the button. <!-- f:chamber_01-f10 -->
5. When it shows the exit-door room, go through to reach the chamberlock. <!-- f:chamber_01-f11 --> Once you're through, you can't go back. <!-- f:chamber_01-f11 -->
6. **Exit.** Test Chamber 01 ends at the chamberlock elevator, and the ride to [Test Chamber 02](#ch-03) is one-way. <!-- f:chamber_01-f02 f:chamber_01-f03 f:chamber_01-f12 -->

> **Tip:** The game saved during the elevator ride in from [Test Chamber 00](#ch-01); both chambers share one map, `testchmb_a_00`. <!-- f:chamber_01-f01 f:chamber_01-f13 --> No save turns up partway through, and you still have no portal gun of your own. <!-- f:chamber_01-f13 f:chamber_01-f01 -->

### Walkthrough

1. Keep your distance from the pellet. It kills on contact, so stay clear of its path until you understand the routing. <!-- f:chamber_06-f06 f:chamber_06-f17 -->
2. Look at the walls. Some are dark brown and cannot take a portal, while the light-grey panels are portalable. <!-- f:chamber_06-f07 f:chamber_06-f16 -->
3. Find the pellet launcher. A fixed orange portal sits beneath it, and the pellet it fires kills instantly on contact. <!-- f:chamber_06-f08 -->
4. Find the pellet receiver by the red light it casts on the ceiling. That light marks where a portal needs to go. <!-- f:chamber_06-f09 f:chamber_06-f16 -->
5. Place a blue portal across from the receiver so the pellet exits aimed at it. Use the red ceiling light as your aiming guide. <!-- f:chamber_06-f09 f:chamber_06-f17 -->
6. Pellets recharge their lifespan each time they pass through a portal, which gives you more time before they disintegrate. <!-- f:chamber_06-f17 -->
7. Before you leave, get the radio above the launcher. Fire a portal on the ceiling near it, grab the radio through the portal, and carry it to the victory lift. <!-- f:chamber_06-f10 f:chamber_06-f04 -->
8. Ride the victory lift to the chamberlock. The captured pellet powers it, and the lift is one-way. <!-- f:chamber_06-f11 -->

### The GLaDOS fight

9. Shortly after you enter, GLaDOS drops a **Morality Core** (purple). Incinerate it: press the red button and drop the core through a portal into the incinerator. This starts a **6-minute neurotoxin countdown** and activates a rocket sentry (the rocket turret). <!-- f:escape_seq-f23 -->
10. Carry the morality core to the incinerator, press the switch, and incinerate it. This triggers the neurotoxin countdown. [†](#note-ch-21-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:escape_seq-f17 -->

12. This is the intended defeat method: redirect the rocket turret's rockets through a portal into GLaDOS to dislodge each remaining core. > **Tip:** There is a faster exploit. A single live turret's bullets also damage GLaDOS. # Appendices {#appendices}

## Items and objects {#app-items .first-in-part}

Portal has a minimal item set. There is no inventory, no drops, and no persistent carrying of items between chambers. Items here are the one tool and the notable objects you interact with. <!-- f:app-items-f01 -->

### The portal gun

Portal's sole interactive tool. It is not a combat weapon, but it is the only item occupying the "weapon/tool" slot. The physics underneath it live in [How the game works](#app-mechanics). <!-- f:app-items-f74 -->

The Aperture Science Handheld Portal Device (the "portal gun") fires two linked portals, blue and orange. Only one of each colour exists at a time; firing a new blue portal moves the existing blue one. <!-- f:app-items-f75 -->

You get it in two stages. The blue-only version comes in [Test Chamber 02](#ch-03); the dual-portal (orange) version comes in [Test Chamber 11](#ch-12). <!-- f:app-items-f02 -->

The **blue-only** device is retrieved from a pit in [Test Chamber 02](#ch-03) (via automated portals). The fully powered **dual-portal** device is acquired in [Test Chamber 11](#ch-12), and picking up the orange portion triggers the "Lab Rat" achievement. [Test Chamber 00](#ch-01) and [Test Chamber 01](#ch-02) have no player-held gun. <!-- f:app-items-f76 -->

A portal attaches only to flat, light-coloured, concrete-type surfaces. Dark metallic brown or black panels, glass, and moving surfaces reject portals (see [How the game works](#app-mechanics)). <!-- f:app-items-f78 -->

The gun passes freely through Material Emancipation Grills, but doing so resets (removes) both active portals and shakes the gun. <!-- f:app-items-f79 -->

## Story notes {#app-story}

The story beats from GLaDOS, in game order. This page holds story spoilers, so stop here if you want to find them out yourself. For where each one happens, follow the chamber links.

### GLaDOS beats

**Early chambers.** GLaDOS is encouraging and clinical, and she keeps promising cake as the reward for completing testing. <!-- f:app-story-f02 -->

**Foreshadowing in [Test Chamber 13](#ch-14).** Instead of promising a reward, she says "When the testing is over, you will be... missed."[†](#note-app-story-medium-confidence){.shaky title="Sources rate this as medium confidence: likely right, not fully confirmed."} <!-- f:app-story-f03 -->

**"The cake is a lie."** This is graffiti left by Doug Rattmann in hidden dens, warning that the promised reward is a manipulation. Writers Erik Wolpaw and Chet Faliszek developed the phrase as a plot device, with no thought for its going viral. Kim Swift wrote and drew the cake element and the behind-the-scenes messages. It became a major meme.[†](#note-app-story-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-story-f04 -->

**The Companion Cube, in [Test Chamber 17](#ch-18).** GLaDOS manipulates you into anthropomorphizing the Weighted Companion Cube, then demands its incineration and mocks your attachment. <!-- f:app-story-f05 -->

**The fire-pit reveal ([Test Chamber 19](#ch-20) into [Escape Sequence / Surface Finale](#ch-21)).** GLaDOS tries to burn you alive. After the escape she claims "We pretended we were going to murder you," and tells you to "assume the party escort submission position." <!-- f:app-story-f06 -->

**The boss fight.** Destroying the Morality Core changes her voice, which turns smoother and less robotic, and she floods the facility with neurotoxin. Only the Intelligence Core recites the cake recipe, with its absurd and lethal ingredients. The Morality Core is mute, the Anger Core growls, and the Curiosity Core asks questions.[†](#note-app-story-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-story-f07 -->

**What the cake represents.** It is a false motivational reward, the illusion of a payoff for compliance. A literal cake does appear post-credits, which complicates a strict "no cake" reading. <!-- f:app-story-f08 -->

### Development note

[Test Chamber 08](#ch-09) was originally meant to be the first pellet chamber. Playtesters found too many mechanics at once, so [Test Chamber 06](#ch-07) and [Test Chamber 07](#ch-08) were inserted before it.[†](#note-app-story-medium-confidence){.shaky title="Sources rate this as medium confidence: likely right, not fully confirmed."} <!-- f:app-story-f09 -->

See also [How the game works](#app-mechanics) and [Missables](#app-missables).

## How the game works {#app-mechanics}

How the game's systems work, and what each hazard does. The portal gun itself is covered in [Items and objects](#app-items), and the story side of things is in [Story notes](#app-story).

### Portal 2 mix-ups to avoid

The original Portal does NOT contain Aerial Faith Plates, Thermal Discouragement Beams (lasers), gels, Hard Light Bridges, or Excursion Funnels. Those are all Portal 2. The original game's systems are limited to portals, buttons, Weighted/Companion Cubes, High Energy Pellets, Unstationary Scaffolds, goo, Emancipation Grills, Sentry Turrets, the rocket turret, and personality cores. <!-- f:app-mechanics-f02 -->

### Portals

**Two linked portals.** The ASHPD fires two linked portals, blue and orange. Only one of each color exists at a time, and firing a new blue portal moves the existing blue one. You and objects pass through one aperture and exit the other instantaneously.[†](#note-app-mechanics-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f03 -->

**Which surfaces take a portal.** Portals attach only to flat, light-colored, concrete-type surfaces. Dark metallic brown or black panels are non-portalable, and glass and moving surfaces reject portals.[†](#note-app-mechanics-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f04 -->
The developer commentary reportedly explains the dark colour as a deliberate choice, so non-portalable surfaces are easy to spot from a distance; the major walkthroughs do not mention it.[†](#note-app-mechanics-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f05 -->

**Crosshair feedback.** The crosshair indicator changes colour when it is over a valid portal-placement surface. It is the primary read for spotting portalable surfaces and edge placements.[†](#note-app-mechanics-medium-confidence){.shaky title="Sources rate this as medium confidence: likely right, not fully confirmed."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f06 -->

**When you get the gun.** [Test Chamber 00](#ch-01) and [Test Chamber 01](#ch-02) have no player gun, only automated portals. You retrieve the blue-only ASHPD in [Test Chamber 02](#ch-03). The fully powered dual-portal (blue and orange) device comes in [Test Chamber 11](#ch-12), and it triggers "Lab Rat". <!-- f:app-mechanics-f07 -->

### Momentum and flinging

**The core rule.** Momentum is conserved. Exit speed equals entry speed, redirected along the exit portal's surface normal. GLaDOS states it in [Test Chamber 10](#ch-11): "Momentum, a function of mass and velocity, is conserved between portals. In layman's terms, speedy thing goes in, speedy thing comes out." <!-- f:app-mechanics-f09 -->

**Fall into fling.** Falling into a floor portal and exiting a wall portal flings you horizontally at the speed you reached falling. This underlies most advanced solutions from [Test Chamber 10](#ch-11) onward. <!-- f:app-mechanics-f10 -->

**Engine constants.** Gravity is governed by `sv_gravity`, which defaults to 600 in Portal (Valve's Source default is 800 in other games). `sv_maxvelocity` caps ballistic speed at 3500 per axis, not total magnitude, so diagonal flings can exceed 3500 total.[†](#note-app-mechanics-sources-disagree){.shaky title="Sources disagree on this detail."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f11 -->

**Air-strafe.** Portal 1 allows air-strafing during a fling to bend the arc, with more air control than Portal 2.[†](#note-app-mechanics-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f12 -->

**Edge cases.** On non-parallel portals you are reoriented upright to gravity after exit. The edge-glitch, or portal-ledging, lets a portal sit on otherwise-invalid edges.[†](#note-app-mechanics-medium-confidence){.shaky title="Sources rate this as medium confidence: likely right, not fully confirmed."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f13 -->

**Portal bumping.** Placing a portal onto another bumps the other portal aside, next to the one you just placed. It can be used to skip certain chambers.[†](#note-app-mechanics-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f14 -->

**Speedrun note: Accelerated Back Hopping.** The dominant movement tech is ABH, a Source/OrangeBox glitch replacing the patched-out bunny hop. New speed per perfect hop is about (current speed x 2 - speed limit), and the gain is exponential. Per the SourceRuns Wiki, the speed limit is 165 UPS while crouched in Portal specifically (versus 209 elsewhere), 225 walking, 285 running, and 352 sprinting. It was discovered by Spider-Waffle.[†](#note-app-mechanics-medium-confidence){.shaky title="Sources rate this as medium confidence: likely right, not fully confirmed."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f15 -->

**Falling speed after death.** What happens to fall velocity on death and respawn is not documented in the sources reviewed.[†](#note-app-mechanics-low-confidence){.shaky title="Sources rate this as low confidence: treat it as a lead, not a certainty."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f16 -->

### Emancipation Grills

**What they do.** They vaporize unauthorized objects (cubes, turrets, cameras, radios, rockets), block portal shots, and reset your active portals on pass-through. You and the ASHPD pass through unharmed. <!-- f:app-mechanics-f17 -->

**Pass or dissolve.** The test subject and portal gun pass. Cubes, turrets, cameras, radios, and rockets are vaporized, and a dissolving turret emits a long "owowowow". Portals cannot be placed through or across a grill, and portals on the far side are unreachable. High Energy Pellets are destroyed on contact like other objects. <!-- f:app-mechanics-f18 -->

**One exception.** Some square metal maintenance panels are NOT affected by the grill, a developer oversight.[†](#note-app-mechanics-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f19 -->

**Names.** StrategyWiki calls it the "Material Emancipation Grid", also "emancipation grills".[†](#note-app-mechanics-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f20 -->
GLaDOS says "Grill" in-game.[†](#note-app-mechanics-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f21 -->
The subtitles and the Prima guide reportedly both use "Grid"; this is not confirmed.[†](#note-app-mechanics-one-source){.shaky title="Only one source reports this."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f22 -->

### Hazards

**Sentry Turrets (first seen in [Test Chamber 16](#ch-17)).** They detect your line-of-sight, deploy laser sights, then open fire. You can pick one up with the portal gun and topple it. The entrance glass in [Test Chamber 16](#ch-17) is bulletproof. <!-- f:app-mechanics-f23 -->
Tactics: place a portal behind or under a turret to topple it. For alcove turrets you can't portal under, place a portal on the red X above them and drop a cube, camera, or turret onto it. Waiting for a turret's laser to settle gives more reaction time.[†](#note-app-mechanics-medium-confidence){.shaky title="Sources rate this as medium confidence: likely right, not fully confirmed."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f24 -->

**Toxic goo and acid (first seen in [Test Chamber 08](#ch-09)).** It is an instant-kill floor hazard. [Test Chamber 08](#ch-09) was originally meant to be the first pellet chamber, but playtesters found too many mechanics at once, so [Test Chamber 06](#ch-07) and [Test Chamber 07](#ch-08) were inserted before it.[†](#note-app-mechanics-medium-confidence){.shaky title="Sources rate this as medium confidence: likely right, not fully confirmed."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f25 -->

**High Energy Pellets (first seen in [Test Chamber 06](#ch-07)).** A bouncing energy ball that kills in one hit. Route it through a portal into a receiver to power lifts and doors. The receiver shines a red light on the ceiling to help aim the portal. <!-- f:app-mechanics-f26 -->
Pellets recharge their lifespan each time they pass through a portal, extending the window before they disintegrate. Otherwise they fade out after a few bounces. <!-- f:app-mechanics-f27 -->
A green, non-disintegrating variant of the High Energy Pellet appears in [Test Chamber 19](#ch-20).[†](#note-app-mechanics-medium-confidence){.shaky title="Sources rate this as medium confidence: likely right, not fully confirmed."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f28 -->

**Unstationary Scaffolds (first seen in [Test Chamber 07](#ch-08)).** A moving platform you ride, typically combined with pellet routing.[†](#note-app-mechanics-medium-confidence){.shaky title="Sources rate this as medium confidence: likely right, not fully confirmed."} <!-- f:app-mechanics-f29 -->

### Objects

**Weighted Storage Cubes.** The primary puzzle prop. Put one on a button to hold a door or mechanism open. Cubes can be carried through portals, and they work as height boosts and turret-drop weapons. <!-- f:app-mechanics-f31 -->

**The Weighted Companion Cube ([Test Chamber 17](#ch-18)).** A special named cube that functions identically to a normal Weighted Storage Cube. It must be incinerated in the Incinerator Room to open the chamber exit, which triggers "Fratricide". The story side is in [Story notes](#app-story). <!-- f:app-mechanics-f32 -->

**Buttons.** Large floor buttons hold-to-activate and need a cube or your weight. There are also wall buttons. <!-- f:app-mechanics-f33 -->
